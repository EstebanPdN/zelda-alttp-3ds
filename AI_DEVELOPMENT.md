# How I develop these projects

I use OpenAI Codex a lot during development.
It helps me with research, code analysis, debugging, code changes, tests, and documentation.
It also writes and modifies code.

I want to explain how I use it, what I do myself, and why this method works for me.

## Research and planning

Before I start a project, I research its code, existing work, and technical requirements.
I use Codex to help investigate these areas.

I compare the project with the limits of the target hardware, which is usually the Nintendo 3DS.
This helps me decide whether the project is practical and what problems I will need to solve.

Then I create a roadmap with help from Codex.
I decide the goals, the order of the work, and which changes to add.
I also provide ideas and possible ways to structure the code or solve problems.

## Why I use Codex

I am a young developer, and I work on these projects on my own.
I have studied computer science for more than four years.
I still have a lot to learn, and I want to keep improving my skills.

Doing every task by hand feels like a step backward to me.
I prefer to delegate repeated work that AI can do as well as I can, or sometimes much better.
I still need to understand the changes, make decisions, and test the results.

To me, refusing these tools would feel like writing programs on paper while everyone else uses a laptop.
I would not do that just to prove I am a "real programmer."

Some tasks involve the same change across 20 or 30 files.
I know how to do many of these tasks by hand, but they can take hours.
Codex can often help me complete this repeated work in minutes.

It also helps me examine large logs and memory dumps.
These files can contain useful information about crashes, memory problems, and performance.
I use that information to investigate problems and compare possible causes with what I can reproduce.

This saves time that I can spend studying the code, solving problems, testing changes, and improving the project.
Without this help, the time needed would make many of these projects impractical for me.

These projects are a way for me to learn.
They also let me bring games I want to play to other systems.

## The work I do myself

I make the final decisions about the project.
I decide what to add, what to change, and what is ready to release.

I reproduce problems, run tests, and examine the results.
I also modify code by hand to fix problems or improve its structure.

I create most of the graphics by hand.
I use pixel art and tools such as Photoshop and, more recently, ArtCraft Suite.

I usually do not like the look of AI-generated images.
They often do not fit the style or finish I want.
I prefer to make graphics that belong in the project and work well together.

I consider myself a perfectionist, especially with visual details.
I care about the interface, spacing, colors, and how everything fits together.
I want the result to feel consistent and look good.

## Code organization and documentation

I use written rules to organize the code, documents, and GitHub repositories.
I also use these rules to guide Codex.

I collect this guidance in [Code Atlas](https://code-atlas.estebanpdn.chatgpt.site/).
It contains the methods and references I use to structure, document, and improve my projects.

I document changes, tests, known problems, and the reasons for important decisions.
This helps me understand previous work and continue from it.
It also helps other people understand what is happening in the project.

## What I think about AI and quality

I personally do not consider my ports "AI slop."

I have seen projects with broken features, unfinished interfaces, and poor performance.
Some look as if the AI output received little attention before release.
I understand why people are concerned about that kind of work.

I put time into research, code changes, tests, graphics, and documentation.
The work continues after Codex produces an answer.
I examine the result, make corrections, and test again.

My goal is to publish something that works well and looks good.
I also want to keep fixing and improving it after release.

These projects matter to me.
I use them to learn, and I care about the result.

## Community discussions

You can disagree with my use of AI.
You can also call this method "vibe coding."
I understand that people have different views.

Questions and criticism about the code, tests, and results are welcome.

Please avoid hateful messages, personal attacks, or fights with other community members over the use of AI.
You do not have to agree with my method to treat other people with respect.
